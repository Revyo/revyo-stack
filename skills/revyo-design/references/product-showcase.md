# Showing a software product

For product-led marketing, make the interface and its outcomes the primary evidence.
Establish which workflow the audience should understand and show enough of the
product to explain it: meaningful starting context, a user action, and its resulting
state. Use the chosen reference to understand the presentation and interaction,
while preserving the requested product and brand.

Taste owns the site's art direction. Embedded app screens also benefit from
`frontend-design` and `emil-design-eng` (linked from the
[design router](../SKILL.md)) for their layout and interaction craft.
Read those when composing the demonstration; the surrounding marketing
page does not make its product UI a different domain.

Reuse approved product screens/components where available. For a new product,
create coherent illustrative UI with consistent fictional people, content, times,
and states across related scenes. Keep the important UI readable at the actual
viewport; adapt its composition on mobile instead of shrinking an entire desktop
screenshot until its labels become illegible. Choose the screens and pacing that
explain this product rather than requiring a fixed device frame or feature count.

App deferral still permits local staged demonstrations. Clearly identify them as
previews; make any visible controls either demonstrate a meaningful state change
or honestly describe their availability. For approval-based workflows, show the
pending and approved outcomes without claiming a real message was sent. Do not
connect accounts, provision a backend, or build the deferred app to support a demo.

Verify the main demonstrated journey in the browser, including keyboard access,
reset/replay where applicable, reduced motion, and a narrow viewport. Keep the
brand consistent between the page, product UI, and delivered share assets. The
reference product's assets, integration counts, and capabilities are not evidence
for the new product's claims.
