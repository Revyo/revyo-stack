# Establish or reuse the brand

Read existing brand tokens, logo/wordmark sources, fonts, public assets, metadata,
and relevant product docs. Check for an approved mascot or existing social card
before inventing replacements. A separate concept, stale generated icon, or older
experiment may not represent the live identity.

When working on production previews, confirm the deployment’s repository/commit,
public domain, and active branding. Use a clean checkout when local experiments
would otherwise be overwritten. For a local concept, keep its claims and URLs
explicitly local until a public target is known.

## Existing identity

Carry forward the real mark, color values, type, and visual language. Fix missing
OG assets without treating the request as a rebrand. Preserve original logo and
mascot artwork unless the user requested a change. A reference image informs style
and composition, not permission to copy another product’s identity or subjects.

## Missing identity

Use the actual product name, audience, purpose, and available domain. Choose a
coherent palette, legible typography, recognizable wordmark/icon treatment, and a
visual idea grounded in the product. State reversible assumptions and continue;
ask only for a missing decision that materially blocks the work.

Create the assets needed for the requested scope. A share-card task does not require
a new landing page, blog series, or invented feature. Keep supporting copy concrete
and truthful, without fabricated customer quotes, ratings, savings, or outcomes.

Keep a compact brand decision record and editable asset sources in the project’s
existing documentation/assets location. Review-only/local-only requests remain
within that gate; otherwise finish the authorized workflow without an extra approval
round for routine reversible design decisions.
